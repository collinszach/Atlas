# Product

## Register

product

## Users
Real travelers who treat their travel as a record worth keeping — people who have been to
dozens of countries and want data fidelity, not a social feed. They use Atlas at a desk to
log and review past trips, on the couch to plan the next one, and occasionally mid-trip to
capture a leg. The job: see everywhere you've been at a glance, understand the shape of your
travel history, and decide where to go next with real intelligence behind the choice.

## Product Purpose
Atlas is a personal travel intelligence platform — part archive, part journal, part planner.
It captures where you've been (past), where you are (present), and helps decide where to go
next (future). Success is a traveler opening Atlas instead of scrolling a photo library or a
spreadsheet, because Atlas shows them something neither can: the map of their own life, and a
credible answer to "where next."

## Brand Personality
Mission control meets an explorer's logbook. Precise, earned, quietly authoritative. The voice
of a seasoned navigator, not a travel influencer. Three words: cartographic, exacting, atmospheric.
The interface should feel like instrumentation you trust — every number real, every line on the
map a place you actually stood.

## Anti-references
- Instagram / travel-blog pastel grids. No bright gradients, no rounded-everything, no emoji-as-UI.
- Generic SaaS dashboards: the hero-metric template (big number + gradient accent), identical card
  grids, tiny uppercase tracked eyebrows over every section.
- Booking.com / Expedia transactional density. Atlas is a record, not a checkout.
- Cream/sand/parchment "warm editorial" near-whites. The warmth here is antique gold on deep navy,
  carried by accent and type, never by a beige body.

## Design Principles
1. **The map is the product.** The globe is the home surface and the emotional core; every other
   screen is in service of putting more truth on it.
2. **Data is earned, so show it like it matters.** Distances, nights, countries — present them
   with the gravity of an instrument readout, in mono, precise, never inflated.
3. **Atmosphere over ornament.** Depth, texture, and motion come from cartographic materials
   (graticules, contours, arcs, deep field) — not decorative gradients or glass.
4. **Restraint is the luxury.** Antique gold is rare and meaningful; most of the surface is deep
   navy and ink. Color marks signal (visited / planned / bucket), never decoration.
5. **Legible under any light.** Dark cartographic, but body text always clears 4.5:1 — instruments
   you can read at a glance, not a mood board you squint at.

## Accessibility & Inclusion
WCAG 2.1 AA. Body text ≥4.5:1 on the navy field (the muted slate `#64748b` fails on `#0a0e1a` and
must not carry body copy). Full reduced-motion alternatives for arc/globe animation. Map color
encodings (visited/planned/bucket) must be distinguishable for color-blind users via shape/label,
not hue alone. Full keyboard navigation for the app shell and all forms.
